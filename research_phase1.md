## 2. WP/EP models and state handling
- nflfastR EP: multinomial logistic (XGBoost) predicting next scoring event (7 outcomes: TD/FG/Safety +/- opponent, No_Score) from state: down, ydstogo, yardline_100, seconds remaining in half, score diff? (no), era (2014+ rules), roof/home, timeouts (offense/defense), receive-2H-kickoff indicator. EP = sum of class probs x point values (TD 6.95 with PAT-adjusted). Baldwin/Yurko writeup: https://www.opensourcefootball.com/posts/2020-09-28-nflfastr-ep-wp-and-cp-models/ ; nflscrapR methods: Yurko, Ventura, Horowitz "nflWAR" https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.00998 (also https://github.com/guga31bb/nflfastR-data ). EP uses era effects and no team strength; EPA is therefore league-average-context-neutral (opponent adjusted by regression downstream).
- nflfastR WP (v2 spread model): XGBoost (GBM, monotone constraints) on: score_differential, seconds_remaining (game), half-seconds, ydstogo, yardline_100, down, receive-second-half-kickoff, timeouts remaining, home indicator (spread_line, with `spread_time` = spread x exp(-4*elapsed fraction) so the pregame spread decays with elapsed time). Also a no-spread version (`wp` vs `vegas_wp`). Key point: pregame team strength enters ONLY as time-decaying spread, so full model reproduces market/margin variance. Source: Baldwin link above; also https://www.nflfastr.com/articles/field_descriptions.html .
- Lock & Nettleton (2014, JQAS 10(2)): random forest, 10 predictors, spread included, plus score diff x time combined variable, timeouts, total points; spread and score diff most important variables. [F] https://homepage.divms.uiowa.edu/~dzimmer/sports-statistics/nflwinprob-2023.pdf ; https://dr.lib.iastate.edu/handle/20.500.12876/90548 ; https://www.nessis.org/nessis13/lock.pdf
- Burke (Advanced Football Analytics): EP/WP via empirical lookup by state (down/dist/yardline/score/time) plus logistic smoothing; 2-model approach using 'Markov chain win probability' (Burke's WP model: state-space with pregame spread priors). https://www.advancedfootballanalytics.com/ (archive; defunct) and Brian Burke on 4th down below.
- ESPN WP (Pro football): bespoke model with team strength via pregame (FPI) -> GBM; uses ESPN FPI (SRS-like, Bayesian, EPA-based); also "Win Probability" blog posts: https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/... (not fetched). [K]
- DVOA (Football Outsiders/FTN): per-play success points above league avg by situation (down/distance/field position/score/time), opponent adjusted; garbage time removed (WP threshold); used to forecast margin with regression to the mean (preseason mixed w/ prior-year DVOA weight decay). https://www.ftnfantasy.com/ , https://www.footballoutsiders.com/info/methods
- Pitfalls of fitted WP [F]: plays within one game are dependent -> effective sample size ~56% in simulation, naive bootstrap CI cover 60% vs 90% nominal, early-game large-deficit states poorly estimated. https://arxiv.org/html/2406.16171v4 . "Paradox of blown leads": the loser's max WP averages ~0.69 (SD 0.139); half of losers reach >=66% WP; 645 NFL games with |spread|<2 show mean 0.686 -> a validity benchmark for a simulator (distribution of loser max WP). [F] https://wsb.wharton.upenn.edu/a-paradox-of-blown-leads-rethinking-win-probability-in-football/
- Sim-validation use of these: run GridSim from kickoff, record WP trajectory via nflfastR WP (nflfastR::calculate_win_probability), and compare (a) distribution of loser max WP, (b) # of lead changes, (c) calibration of sim WP at each state vs model WP (should match for even spread).

## 3. Known simulation approaches and pitfalls
- Markov drive models: states = (down, ydstogo, yardline_100) absorbing outcomes (TD, FG, punt, TO, safety). Carter & Machol 1971 (EP), Romer 2006 (4th down), Burke. Pros: fast, matches EP; cons: ignores clock, team heterogeneity, score-dependent play-calling unless extended.
- Play-level MC with team-specific ratings: e.g. PFF/FiveThirtyEight-style (538 Elo: https://fivethirtyeight.com/methodology/how-our-nfl-predictions-work/ -- Elo K=20, HFA 65 elo pts (~2.2 pts, later reduced to ~48), MOV multiplier with ln(|MOV|+1)*2.2/((elo_diff*.001)+2.2), QB adjustment, rest/travel, reversion 1/3 to mean each offseason) -> gives margin only, no play-level.
- Bayesian hierarchical ratings: e.g. Glickman & Stern (1998, state-space for NFL, weekly ratings, HFA, sd of margin ~ 13.5, week-to-week drift), https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1998.10473693 ; Stan implementations; Baio-style off/def Poisson (but NFL scoring not Poisson: scores are multiples 3/7 with correlated/discrete distribution; see Lopez, Matthews, Baumer "How often does the best team win?" AoAS 2018 https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.05976 -- NFL has the most randomness among major sports after MLB/NHL? in their analysis NFL ~ 'best team wins' ~ 0.6-0.7 range; estimated NFL is between... verify).
- Elo/SRS/DVOA: margin-of-victory ratings with ~ 13.2 SD residual (Vegas: SD(margin - spread) ~ 13.1-13.6 historically; Stern 1991 "On the probability of winning a football game" Am Stat 45(3): margin ~ Normal(spread, 13.86); more recent ~ 13.2-13.5). https://doi.org/10.1080/00031305.1991.10475798
- Pitfalls documented / common: (a) treating play outcomes as iid across plays of a team -> variance too low (no persistent per-game latent factor: weather, QB health, game plan, ref crew, turnover luck); (b) double counting HFA when ratings are fit on neutral (adjusted) data but HFA is added to both offense and defense efficiency; (c) not regressing team ratings (EPA/play per-team has year-to-year correlation r ~ 0.4-0.55 for offense/passing, ~0.1-0.3 for defense and turnovers; sacks/turnovers/penalty rates are very noisy; must shrink toward league mean by ~ n/(n+k) with k~ many hundred plays); (d) ignoring game-script feedback (trailing -> pass more, run clock differently; leader -> rush, bleed clock; reduces game-level variance in some ways and increases drive variance in others); (e) possession-count rigidity (clock model too slow/fast; real ~ 11-12 per team) and the 'two-minute drill' / end-of-half timing; (f) ignoring special teams scoring/penalty drive-extending events and variance of starting field position; (g) ignoring blowout garbage time WP rule (starters pulled; run-heavy).
- Public references on sims: https://github.com/nflverse (nflfastR; no official sim); https://pypi.org/project/nflsim ; Winter Simulation Conf 2017 paper https://www.informs-sim.org/wsc17papers/includes/files/062.pdf (football sim w/ state transition; not read in detail) ; Mannion? ; Football Outsiders 'drive success'. [K for those not fetched]

## 5. Fantasy-relevant modeling
- Target share/air yards/opportunity: key predictive stats: (weighted opportunity) = 1.5 x targets + ... ; 'Expected fantasy points' (xFP) from nflverse `ffopportunity` model: https://github.com/ffverse/ffopportunity and https://ffopportunity.ffverse.com/ . Components: air_yards, target depth, RZ/GL carries, yards-to-go, receiver position -> expected yards/TD/FP. Use for receiver stat generation; target share is stable year-to-year (r~0.7-0.8 for WR1/TE alpha) while TD/yard-per-target regress heavily (TD rate r~0.2-0.3).
- Air yards: ADOT per player stable (r~0.7); WOPR = 1.4*tgt_share + 0.7*air_yards_share; RACR; Josh Hermsmeyer/AirYards: https://www.airyards.com/ . nflverse columns: `air_yards`, `yards_after_catch`, `xyac_*` (xYAC model), `cpoe` (CP model).
- Red-zone usage: RB share of inside-5 carries volatile; pass targets inside the 10 concentrate on 1-2 players (TE/WR1) share ~ 40% of RZ tgts to WR/TE alpha; goal-to-go carries: QB sneak share rising (Eagles/Bills/Bengals) -> QB rush TDs.
- RB role splits: early-down/third-down/two-minute/goal-line usage; rush share ~ 55-75% for lead backs, receiving back targets ~ 15-20% of RB targets concentrating on pass-down backs; model snap share x role by down/distance/WP state; 3rd-down backs get ~ 65-80% of 3rd-and-long snaps.
- QB scrambles: scrambles ~ 3.5-4% of dropbacks league-wide (rushing QB scrambles in nflfastR `qb_scramble`), designed QB rush ~ 5-6% of rush attempts; mobile QBs (Lamar/Jalen/Daniels/Allen) rush 8-12 att/g; scramble yds ~ 6-7 ypc, EPA ~ +0.3-0.4/play (very high; scrambles are +EPA vs sacks).

- Correlation structure: team total + spread -> QB/WR1 correlated; sack/turnover rate; garbage-time.

## 6. Common simulator failure modes (with targets)
1. Score distribution shape: real NFL scores are lumpy; individual score support at 0,3,6,7,... ; scores ~ team points mean ~22-23, SD ~ 9.5-10 pts per team; total points SD ~ 13-14 around the market total (market total residual SD ~ 10-10.5; actual totals SD ~ 13.5); frequent margins 3 and 7 (3.5%/ 5% of games each); margin-of-victory distribution Normal-ish SD 13.2-13.5 around spread with fat tail at |margin| >= 21 ~ 12-15% of games... ; key numbers: 3 (~8%), 7 (~6%), 10, 6, 14. ~ 0.5% ties.
2. Variance: sim margin SD must equal ~ 13.0-13.5 around spread for a pair with spread known; play-level iid sims typically yield 11-12 (too low) if no game-level latent factors and if per-play outcome variance is under-modeled; or too high if ratings are not regressed. Calibrate: simulate N games for (spread=0) -> SD(margin) 13.2-13.5, SD(total) ~ 13.5-14.5, mean total ~ 45-46 (2021-24 average ~ 45-46.5).
3. Possession counts: ~ 11-12 per team (not 10 or 14); possession counts correlated with pace/run rate; end-of-half possessions; unrealistic seconds/play (should be 28-30 avg). Fewer possessions -> higher variance; check sim distribution of possessions by game.
4. Mean reversion/shrinkage: team ratings from small samples (17 games ~ 1000 plays/side) must be shrunk; for EPA/play off: shrink factor ~ 0.4-0.55 after 17 games; for defense ~ 0.2-0.3; for turnovers/fumbles ~ 0.05-0.1; for sack rate offense ~ 0.3-0.4 (OL), defense ~ 0.2 ; for red zone TD% ~ 0.1; for penalty rates ~ 0.3 (team) ; for pass rate (PROE) ~ 0.6-0.7 (coach style persistent). Use hierarchical priors + preseason prior (Elo reversion 1/3 to mean; Glickman-Stern 2 drift). Also regress rating the season-to-season: SRS next-year r ~ 0.4-0.5 (-> 0.5 shrink).
5. HFA double counting: if team strength was computed from home/away-unadjusted data and then HFA added at point-differential level AND play-level offense/defense efficiency, effective HFA double (e.g. 2.5 x 2). Target: total ~1.5-2.0 pts margin; ~ 53-54% win for evenly matched. Split HFA between offense/defense/special teams or inject only into margin-latent factor. Use [F] 1.73 pts (0.65-pt decline per 20 years) and 53.3% [F] home win 2024.
6. Blowout behavior: real: leading team shifts run-heavy and plays prevent/soft zones; trailing team passes more/hurry-up/go for it on 4th; at WP>=95% garbage time -> starters out, kneel downs; simulate: sim scoring stops being 'efficient' after lead >14 in 4Q; proportion of games with margin >=21 ~ 12-14%, >=28 ~ 4-6%. If sim has no garbage time, blowout frequency too high or low; if play-calling doesn't depend on score, losing teams' comeback rate too high.
7. Correlated game-level latent factors: needed for correct (a) total variance, (b) margin variance, (c) team offensive correlation: weather (wind/cold -> pass efficiency -6..-10%), pace factor (shared by both teams; correlation of team plays ~ +0.3-0.4), game-flow/script factor, QB injury/out-in-game (~ 5% games/start), injuries/inactives, referee crew penalty rate (crew-level effect SD ~ 1.5 pen/game), turnover luck (negative binomial rather than Poisson: overdispersion), special-teams random events (return TDs ~ 1% of games), turnover recovery ~ 50% fumbles recovered by defense etc. Typical fix: sample game-level multipliers from lognormal (pace, efficiency) before each game, shared between teams.
8. Other: wrong discretization of yards (heavy-tail: pass plays 20+ ~ 9%, 40+ ~ 1.5%; skew of yardage distribution: mean 5.5 but median ~3-4); first-down distance distribution; 'drive success' not matching EPA; 4th-down policy fixed at nfl4th optimum (too aggressive); end-of-clock behaviors off; kicker model ignoring distance, wind, dome; punting (net 41-42 yds; touchback rate ~ 7-8% of punts; inside-20 rate ~ 36-40%); return TDs; PATs; no 2-pt logic; no penalty logic (spot, replay down, auto 1st); 'garbled' states: first down at goal line, goal-to-go distance; safeties; fumbles out of end zone; onside kicks; OT state.
9. ## 7. Ranked list: biggest gaps for a typical play-level sim vs this research
1. No game-level correlated latent factors (pace, efficiency/weather, script, QB state, officials) -> margin/total SD too low (~11-12 vs 13.2-13.5). Highest impact on every spread/total/prop output.
2. No hierarchical shrinkage/regression to the mean of team and player rates (esp. defense, turnovers, sacks, RZ%, penalties); raw season EPA used as truth -> overdispersed team spreads, over-confident favorites.
3. Score-state-dependent play calling missing/weak (pass rate vs WP, hurry-up, kneels, garbage time, prevent) -> wrong blowout/comeback rates, wrong possessions, wrong fantasy volumes.
4. Clock/possession calibration (seconds/play by outcome, 2-min logic, end-of-half, timeouts) -> possessions/game off vs ~ 11-12 per team; plays ~ 63/team.
5. HFA specification: must be ~1.7-2.0 pts total, applied once; old 2.5-3.0 or double-applied overstates home win% (53-54% actual [F]).
6. 4th-down policy: needs team/coach aggressiveness near modern nfl4th (go rate ~ 71% on 4th&1 [F]); using pre-2018 conservatism or full-optimal both wrong; need FG model by distance (50+ ~ 70% [F]) and decision-by-WP.
7. Rules/era: 2024-25 kickoff (touchback 30/35 variants, return rate +57% [F]), OT (10-min, both possess [F]; ties), PAT/2-pt, penalty types; fixed rule era mix.
